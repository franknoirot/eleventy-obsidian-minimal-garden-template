One of the problems of progressivism is that—and this is a good thing—nearly none of our enemies are truly people, places, or things, but systems. Even the worst opposers of an ecological humanist future are usually at worst reactionaries operating the dams at the leverage points of society (Hon. Alito and Roberts come to mind).

That healthy new way of thinking about conflicts at the heart of societal change doesn't sit well in our human brains though. For me and people like me, it tends to result in a vague "global ache" that, if left untreated will become nihilism, although a strange new *pro*-anthropic form of nihilism.

My limited primate brain needs, at least for now, *objects* of my frustration.

## How to pick
For this reason, I propose anyone with leftist politics *choose a nemesis*. Find a system that seems big and daunting and set on the wrong tracks and identify either one **agent** or one **leverage point** within it to focus your efforts on. We can continue to recognize and remind ourselves that our nemesis is but a point on the graph of inadequacy, or a player darting along its webbed game, but at least we have something for our eyes to follow. Without a point or two to focus on, my vision tends to blur and the webs that bind us begin to look like walls.

This nemesis can be anything you like. We must remember that we, specifically our individual selves, do not and cannot confront the misanthropy of the world alone. We can only carve out a bit of it and try to heal, and continue to communicate both that we are continually trying, and what is working and what is not.

So pick a pet nemesis: get specific enough for the nemesis to only really be right for you. In general, *do not* pick individual people as your systems nemesis. That will pretty inevitably draw you away from a systems analysis mindset into a...stalking one. No, just like we as individuals will not be the progenitors of progress, neither will frustrating an individual trying to hold onto unjust forms of power[^1].

Instead, pick an agent or policy in a system that is bad precisely because it is missing humanity in it. Here are some other possible suggestions:
- a corporation that has flown under the public radar for too long
- a council in your town that is either not governing or governing against its constituents
- a policy at your local prison that makes the families of prisoners pay exorbitant fees to call them

## What to do with a nemesis
Up to this point this might sound like a field guide to activism. Nope. I want all of us who want a better world to *build the muscle of pointing out bad things*. So all I want you to do with your nemesis is:
1. call them out as your nemesis (to at least one other person)
2. research them publicly
3. share your findings and your opinions as you go.

I hear a lot of frustration about the big-ticket baddies on my timeline, but not enough about specific, contingent things that aren't on the dockets of major news corporations. We have been talking about the death of the news for decades, and now the misinformation of social media for at least 5 years, so now it's time to try and work on that problem.

Identify a thing you don't like about the state of the world, research it and share it as you go, then report about it. I will be working on [[Coca Cola Research|Coca Cola]] for a bit for practice, because I don't know where they came from, how they operate, or how they ended up with so much control over water supplies in other countries. 

While I do have some hope that someone's nemesis research will result in direct action and change, I am more interested in the possibility that everyone having a nemesis will create a culture of casual institutional surveillance. The vast majority of our lives is spent being monitored by institutions—mostly surreptitiously—and I think we should cultivate some bottom-up watchfulness to counterweight it.

It doesn't have to be a big thing. Just look up a few operational details about a company or government program you've got a queasy feeling about every once in a while. Here, I'll start: Coca-Cola was founded in 1886 in Atlanta, GA, where it is still headquartered today.
